# Subjunctive Basics

The subjunctive is not a tense — it's a mood. It signals *unreality, doubt, emotion, or desire about something*.

## The first three triggers to internalize

### 1. Desire / requests (querer, pedir, esperar)

> *Quiero que repitas.* — I want you to repeat.
> *Espero que llegue pronto.* — I hope he arrives soon.

### 2. Emotion (me alegra que, me sorprende que, qué bueno que)

> *Me alegra que estés aquí.* — I'm glad you're here.
> *Qué bueno que vinieras.* — How great that you came.

### 3. Doubt or denial (no creo que, dudo que, no es cierto que)

> *No creo que esté abierto.* — I don't think it's open.
> *Dudo que tenga cambio.* — I doubt he has change.

## The form

For *-ar* verbs, swap the ending to *-e* endings.
For *-er* and *-ir* verbs, swap to *-a* endings.

| Verb | yo present | yo subjunctive |
|---|---|---|
| hablar | hablo | hable |
| comer | como | coma |
| vivir | vivo | viva |
| tener | tengo | tenga |
| poder | puedo | pueda |
| ir | voy | vaya |
| ser | soy | sea |
| estar | estoy | esté |

## Polite-request tie-in

The polite *quisiera* (see [[Conditional and Polite Requests]]) is technically the imperfect subjunctive of *querer*. That's why it sounds softer — it's grammatically tentative.

## Voice note

Don't try to "use the subjunctive." Listen for the triggers (*quiero que, espero que, no creo que, qué bueno que*) and let the form follow.
