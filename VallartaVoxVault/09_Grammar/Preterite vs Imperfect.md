# Preterite vs Imperfect

Spanish has two past tenses. They don't mean "long ago" vs "recently" — they encode different *kinds* of past.

## Preterite — what happened

A finished action with a clear boundary.

- *Comí tacos al pastor.* — I ate al pastor tacos. *(specific event)*
- *Fui al Malecón.* — I went to the Malecón.
- *Hablamos por una hora.* — We talked for an hour. *(boundary: one hour)*

## Imperfect — what was happening / what used to be

Ongoing, habitual, background, scene-setting.

- *Comía tacos al pastor cada viernes.* — I used to eat al pastor tacos every Friday.
- *Iba al Malecón cuando vi la lluvia.* — I was going to the Malecón when I saw the rain.
- *Había una luz en la ventana.* — There was a light in the window. *(scene)*

## The pairing

Most past stories use **both**: imperfect for the background, preterite for the events that happen against it.

> *Caminaba por la playa cuando vi un perro grande.*
> *I was walking on the beach when I saw a big dog.*

Imperfect = camera panning. Preterite = camera cutting.

## High-frequency imperfect forms

- **era / estaba / había** — *was* (identity / state / existence)
- **tenía** — *had*
- **iba** — *was going / used to go*
- **veía** — *was seeing / used to see*

## Voice note

Magical realism (Borges, García Márquez, Rulfo) lives in the imperfect. The imperfect is how Spanish tells you "this just kept being true." See [[Magical Realism]].
