# Trail: *arrabal* — from al-Andalus to the edges of Buenos Aires to the colonias of PV

A walk through one word. Five nodes, four edges. Read top to bottom; the edges are why the nodes belong on the same trail.

---

## Node 1 — Andalusi Arabic *ar-rabaḍ*

The outer quarter of a walled medieval city. The neighborhoods outside the walls, where the city overflowed. One of roughly 4,000 Spanish words inherited from 800 years of al-Andalus.

> **Source:** RAE etymological entry, dle.rae.es/arrabal

## Edge — phonological + cultural transfer

The Arabic article *al-* + noun *rabaḍ* enters Old Spanish as a single lexicalized word with the Arabic article fused on. Same pattern as *alcalde, almohada, alfombra, azúcar, aceite*. The *ar-* is not Spanish — it's frozen Arabic.

## Node 2 — Old Spanish *arrabal*

By the 13th century the word is naturalized. It still means *outer quarter*, but the walls it refers to are now Iberian Christian city walls. The word survives the *Reconquista* with its meaning intact.

## Edge — semantic drift toward edge-as-character

By the colonial period, *arrabal* picks up a second meaning: not just the geographic outer quarter, but the *kind of place* outer quarters become — informal, poorer, full of recent arrivals, where the city's official self thins out. The word starts carrying register.

## Node 3 — Buenos Aires *arrabal* (Borges)

Borges uses *arrabal* not for slums but for the **liminal zones where the city meets the pampa**. The arrabal of *Fervor de Buenos Aires* is Palermo in 1923 — a neighborhood where unpaved streets, *almacenes*, and patios full of two or three colors are still close enough to the country to be confused with it.

The poem *Calle desconocida* names the dove's-half-light first; the *arrabal* arrives a few lines later. Borges always sets up *arrabal* with an inherited name first — as if the word needs the prior Arabic to land right.

> **Cross-reference:** `06_Atelier/Borges/calle-desconocida.md`

## Edge — colonial export to Mexico

*Arrabal* crosses to colonial Mexico with the same liminal-zone meaning. Mexican Spanish keeps it but uses it less than Argentine Spanish — Mexico prefers *colonia* for neighborhood and reserves *arrabal* for the more pejorative "rough outskirts." Useful to know if you're using Borges as a model for Mexican Spanish: the *arrabal* register doesn't translate cleanly across the Atlantic.

## Node 4 — Puerto Vallarta *colonia*

PV doesn't say *arrabal*. It says *colonia*. *Colonia Pitillal, Colonia Versalles, Colonia 5 de Diciembre*. The arrabales of PV are the colonias up the hill from the malecón, where the cobblestone runs out and the cement starts. The phenomenology Borges names — the edge of the city dissolving into something else — is alive in PV; the word is different.

If you want to *bridge* Borges into Vallarta, use *colonia* and write the same kind of sentence:

> *Por la colonia del cerro se cansaron los dos o tres colores del muro.*

## Edge — to your own writing

This is the trail's open end. You have:

- The Arabic root (`ar-rabaḍ`) — the etymological cave.
- The Borges register (Buenos Aires *arrabal*) — the literary face.
- The Mexican equivalent (*colonia*) — the operational face.

The Cube edge here is **Influential** (Borges-lens) → **Situational** (PV ground) → **Variation** (write the line yourself, with *colonia*). Three edges, one move per edge.
