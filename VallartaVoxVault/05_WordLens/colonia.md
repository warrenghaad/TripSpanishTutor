# *colonia*

**Part of speech:** feminine noun
**CEFR:** A2
**Register:** neutral, everyday Mexican Spanish

## Meaning

A residential neighborhood. The standard Mexican word for what an Argentine would call a *barrio* and a *porteño* poet would call an *arrabal*. PV addresses include *Colonia* before the neighborhood name — *Colonia Pitillal*, *Colonia Versalles*, *Colonia 5 de Diciembre*, *Colonia Emiliano Zapata* — the last one is where most of the malecón restaurants live.

## In a taxi

- *Lléveme a la Colonia 5 de Diciembre, por favor.*
- *¿Esta colonia es la 5 de Diciembre o la Versalles?* — the boundary is around Calle Aldama.

## Etymology

From Latin *colonia*, a settled place — same root as English *colony*. In Mexican Spanish the word lost the colonial-administrative weight English keeps; *colonia* in Mexico is just *neighborhood*.

## Bridge to Borges

Borges's *arrabal* and PV's *colonia* point at the same phenomenology — the edge of the city, the place where the official street grid thins. The words sit on opposite ends of register: *arrabal* is literary and elegiac, *colonia* is administrative and neutral. To write a Borges-shaped sentence about PV, use *colonia* and let the imagery do the work the word's register would have done in Buenos Aires.
