# *arrabal*

**Part of speech:** masculine noun
**CEFR:** B1
**Register:** literary in Argentine Spanish; mildly pejorative in Mexican Spanish

## Meaning

The outer quarter of a city — the neighborhoods beyond the historical walls or beyond the official center. The edge zone where the city dissolves into something else.

## Etymology trail

| Era | Form | Gloss |
|---|---|---|
| Andalusi Arabic | *ar-rabaḍ* | the outer quarter, suburb |
| Old Spanish | *arrabal* | naturalized; *al-* article frozen onto the noun |
| Argentine usage | *arrabal* | literary, slightly elegiac — Borges's preferred register |
| Mexican usage | *arrabal* | mildly pejorative; *colonia* is the everyday word |

## Borges anchor

> *…por las calles del arrabal…*

Borges uses *arrabal* not for slums but for the **liminal zones where Buenos Aires meets the pampa** — Palermo in 1923, unpaved, almacenes on corners, patios with two or three colors.

See: `06_Atelier/Borges/calle-desconocida.md`, `04_Trails/arrabal-etymology.md`.

## In Vallarta

Don't use *arrabal* in PV. Use *colonia*. *Colonia Pitillal, Colonia Versalles, Colonia 5 de Diciembre*. If you want the literary weight Borges puts on *arrabal*, you have to import it via simile — *la colonia tenía algo de arrabal porteño* — or quote-mark it.
