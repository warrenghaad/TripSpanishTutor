# CUBE — The Geometry

The conceptual engine of Vallarta Vox / TripSpanishTutor. The Cube is **not a curriculum**. It is the geometry the writer's actual sentences inhabit. Everything else in this vault — Templates, prompts, day-packs, the Atelier — is a way of inviting the writer to a face, an edge, or an axis they have not yet sat on.

Companion files: [`PRISM.md`](PRISM.md) (how the Cube refracts under attention) and [`../12_Schemas/prompts/cube-emergence-rule.md`](../12_Schemas/prompts/cube-emergence-rule.md) (the single rule that generates the next prompt). Theoretical audit lives in `CUBE-prism-vox.md` at repo root.

---

## 1. The center

The center is **a Spanish sentence, phrase, or word** that the writer has just produced or just landed on. The writer is always *at* a center. Every prompt acts on the current center; no prompt is issued otherwise.

A sentence is a sufficient diagnostic unit (Hunt's T-unit; Foster-Tonkyn-Wigglesworth's AS-unit). One sentence reveals proficiency under the CAF framework (Complexity-Accuracy-Fluency).

The center has two simultaneous readings:
- **Proficiency-reading** — where the writer is, against PCIC inventories, CAF measures, and Spanish diagnostic windows (aspect, mood, clitics, *ser*/*estar*, register).
- **Tension-reading** — what the sentence wants next: which Sentence Engine slots are empty, which edges are open but untraversed, which faces have not yet been sat on for this center.

Halliday's clause-as-message (ideational / interpersonal / textual metafunctions) and Bakhtin's addressivity — "the quality of turning to someone" — are the operative descriptions of what "a sentence" means here.

---

## 2. The eight faces

A face is a **place of attention** the writer can sit on for the current center. Faces are not stages and have no order. The Learning face is a face, not a meta-layer.

| Face | What the writer attends to | Typical prompt shape |
|---|---|---|
| **Perception** | What is being seen, heard, smelled, felt on the body | "What sense is loudest in this sentence?" |
| **Generation** | Producing the surface utterance | "Say it once more, faster, no rewriting" |
| **Variation** | Copia — the same meaning, in another shape | "Say it again with a different verb of motion" |
| **Depth** | Etymology, image-logic, the cave under the word | "Where does this word come from?" |
| **Situation** | The phenomenology of the speech act — orientation, pressure, listener | "Who is being addressed? With what pressure?" |
| **Memory** | The Reliquary — saved nodes and edges with retrieval affordances | "Have you said something like this before?" |
| **Spoken-Form** | Mouth, breath, rhythm, audibility | "Read it aloud — where does the rhythm break?" |
| **Learning** | Retrospective comparison across the writer's recent graph | "Compare the verb here to the verb three sentences back" |

The Learning face is **available, not mandatory**. A writer working in Spanish to *write* — not to learn — can stay on Perception → Generation → Variation indefinitely. This is what distinguishes a writing instrument from a course.

## 3. Typed edges

An edge is a **move from one node to another**. Edges are typed. A move is never "do the next thing"; it is always "traverse this specific kind of edge."

| Edge type | What it does | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Structural** | Rearranges the clause without changing meaning | active → passive; theme-rheme reorder |
| **Transformational** | Changes tense, mood, aspect, or modality | preterite → imperfect; indicative → *como si* counterfactual |
| **Situational** | Re-grounds the sentence in another addressee or pressure | private journal voice → speaking to the gate agent |
| **Influential** | Re-voices through an author lens | the sentence as Borges would compress it; as Rulfo would haunt it; as Paz would press it |

Influential edges are **never goals**. They are probes. Paz: *"el estilo es el punto de partida de todo intento creador… cuando un poeta adquiere un estilo, una manera, deja de ser poeta."* Style is a departure point. The Cube must never push a Borgesian rewrite as a destination — only as a move that helps the sentence find what it didn't know it wanted.

Bloom's six revisionary ratios — *clinamen* (swerve), *tessera* (antithetical completion), *kenosis* (self-emptying), *daemonization* (counter-sublime), *askesis* (self-purgation), *apophrades* (return of the dead) — operate as **second-order edge types** on the Influential edge. They are available moves, not stages.

---

## 4. Vertices

A vertex is a **convergence point** where multiple edges meet. Vertices accumulate weight; a sentence that sits at a high-vertex location (many open edges, several already traversed) is a candidate "core sentence" for the writer's emerging *figura* (Cortázar). The graph does not need to label these — they emerge from traversal density.

---

## 5. The three axes

Axes are **directions of movement**, not places. A single move can have a primary axis and a secondary one.

| Axis | Direction | What it measures |
|---|---|---|
| **Vertical** | Grammatical complexity | T-unit length, subordination depth, marked aspect/mood, clause-combining; the CAF *complexity* dimension |
| **Lateral** | Register and voice | Familiar → formal; Mexican → Argentine → Peninsular; private → public; the CEFR Companion Volume's mediation dimension |
| **Depth** | Etymology, metaphor, the cave | Surface → root; literal → figural; mundane → mythic |

Boden's three creative types map onto the axes:
- **Combinational** creativity is Lateral (familiar elements, unfamiliar combinations).
- **Exploratory** creativity is Vertical (traversal of a defined conceptual space).
- **Transformational** creativity is Depth (locally changing which constraints govern the word).

---

## 6. What the Cube is *not*

It is not a curriculum. There are no levels. The seven "stages" in earlier drafts (Surface fluency, Variation, Expansion, Spelunking, Transfer, Voice, Memory) **are six faces and one axis dressed as a sequence**. The Cube drops staging language entirely.

It is not a Borges-finishing-school. Influential edges to Borges, Rulfo, Paz, Neruda, Cortázar, are equiprobable; the engine fires whichever the sentence affords.

It is not optimized for "the writer learned X grammar point." If the engine ever optimizes for that over "the writer wrote what they meant," it has betrayed its purpose.

---

## 7. The graph is the learner model

The writer's actual sentence-graph — the nodes they have produced, the edges they have traversed — *is* the learner model. There is no hidden vector, no skill-meter, no opaque probability table. The writer can literally see their own learner model because it is their own writing rendered as a graph (Bull & Kay's Open Learner Model, taken to its limit).

Three claims collapse the architecture:
1. **Writing is the assessment.** Dynamic Assessment (Lantolf & Poehner) dissolves the diagnose/teach split.
2. **Proficiency is not separable from what the writer has done.** Any abstract representation is lossy compression of the text.
3. **Open learner models are pedagogically superior when readable.** The graph *is* the open learner model.

---

## 8. Three sources of constraint

The Cube reads the current center against three references — never one alone:

1. **PCIC** (Plan Curricular del Instituto Cervantes, 2006) — twelve inventories × five components × six CEFR levels. Read pluricentrically: PCIC's stated Spain-norm preference is a default, not a prescription; the writer's region of use (Vallarta = Mexican) is the operative norm.
2. **The Sentence Engine** — Perception + Motion + Modifier + Emotional Pressure + Relation, mapped to Halliday's three metafunctions. The Engine is a *default lens*, not universal; transactional and expository sentences need their own lenses (see PRISM §3).
3. **The graph itself** — the writer's traversal history. Novelty and ZPD are read off the graph, not assumed.

## 9. Failure modes the geometry guards against

| Risk | Guard |
|---|---|
| Emergent prompting becomes random | Tension-score is calibrated to Sentence Engine slot-emptiness and edge-untraversedness — both objectively computable |
| Writer plateaus | Plateau detector: if last *k* sentences have not increased CAF complexity, raise tension threshold and bias toward avoided edges |
| False immersion | Only typed `GLOSS_FALLBACK` edges permit English; marked, time-bound, expiring |
| Literary over-reach | `face_underuse` forces visits to Situation, Spoken-Form; Influential edges fire only when the sentence exhibits the technique-affordance |
| New curriculum under emergent dressing | Strict prohibition on global session shapes. The system has no plan for the session — only a rule for the next move |
| Voice erasure | Voice-fingerprint markers (preferred connectives, characteristic adjective density, recurring metaphor families) are *protected*; surface as flagged any move that would erase a voice marker rather than develop it |
| Graph becomes unwieldy | Working set = recent *n* + flagged-as-important + reachable in *m* edges from current center. The Memory face handles full retrieval |
| System trains the writer in its own ontology | The geometry stays mostly implicit. The writer experiences prompts, not edge-type names |

---

## 10. The single architectural commitment

> **The graph of sentences the writer has actually produced, with edges actually traversed, becomes the learner model.**

This collapses three problems into one — proficiency tracking, creative-writing prompting, and Spanish-pedagogy sequencing. None of these need to be solved independently when the graph is correctly structured.

The Cube is the geometry. The [PRISM](PRISM.md) is how the Cube refracts under the writer's attention right now. The [emergence rule](../12_Schemas/prompts/cube-emergence-rule.md) is the only operation.
