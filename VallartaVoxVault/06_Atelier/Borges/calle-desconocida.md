> Penumbra de la paloma
> llamaron los hebreos a la iniciación de la tarde.

## Meaning
The Hebrews called the beginning of evening "the dove's half-light." The poem opens with an act of naming — a borrowed name — and immediately the calle becomes a site of inherited language.

## Literal
*Penumbra / de-la-paloma / llamaron / los-hebreos / a-la-iniciación / de-la-tarde.*
"Half-light / of-the-dove / called / the-Hebrews / to-the-initiation / of-the-evening."

## Natural
"The dove's half-light, the Hebrews called the beginning of evening."

## Grammar Skeleton
Inverted-word-order naming construction: `[Object-name] llamaron [Subject] a [Thing-named]`. Spanish places the *name* first for poetic emphasis, then the namer, then the named thing — three nominal phrases stitched by one verb. In conversation you would say *Los hebreos llamaron «penumbra de la paloma» a la iniciación de la tarde.* The poem's inversion is doing work.

This is also the poem where Borges drops *arrabal* a few lines later — from Andalusi Arabic *ar-rabaḍ* (the outer quarter). Buenos Aires's edges keep the al-Andalus inheritance in their name.

## Practice Move
Use the inversion: `[Name] llamaron [los X] a [Thing].` *"Hijos del barrio" llamaron los porteños a los que crecieron en el arrabal.* *"Hora azul" llamaron los franceses al momento entre el día y la noche.* Three attempts.
