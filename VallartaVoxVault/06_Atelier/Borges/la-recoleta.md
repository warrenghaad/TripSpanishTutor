> Convencidos de caducidad
> por tantas nobles certidumbres del polvo

## Meaning
Convinced of our own expiration by so many noble certainties of dust. The speaker walks the Recoleta cemetery and finds the certainty of death rendered *noble* — not because death is noble, but because the dust has been arranged with such gravity that doubt is no longer possible.

## Literal
*Convencidos / de-caducidad / por-tantas / nobles / certidumbres / del-polvo.*
"Convinced / of-expiration / by-so-many / noble / certainties / of-the-dust."

## Natural
"Convinced of our own expiration by so many noble certainties of dust."

## Grammar Skeleton
Past participle as adjective-of-state (`convencidos`) modifying an unstated *nosotros*. The whole phrase is an unmoored fragment — no main verb. This is the **adverbial absolute** construction Spanish allows freely: a participial phrase functioning as a complete affective stance. English approaches it with "Convinced as we were…" but Spanish lets the participle stand alone.

Note `caducidad` — the noun for *expiring, lapsing, going out of force*. Latin *cadere*, to fall. The same root as *cadence* in music, *cascade* in geography. Borges chose the noun that already meant *falling* before it meant *dying*.

## Practice Move
Use the bare-participle adverbial absolute: `Convencidos de X por tantas Y de Z.` *Convencidos de pequeñez por tantas estrellas de la noche.* *Convencidos de continuidad por tantos rezos de la abuela.* Hold the structure; vary the nouns.
