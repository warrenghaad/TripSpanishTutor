> Con la tarde
> se cansaron los dos o tres colores del patio.

## Meaning
With the evening, the two or three colors of the patio grew tired. The patio is small enough to *have* two or three colors, and the evening exhausts them — light is the agent, the patio is the patient, exhaustion is the verb.

## Literal
*Con-la-tarde / se-cansaron / los-dos-o-tres / colores / del-patio.*
"With-the-evening / themselves-tired-out / the-two-or-three / colors / of-the-patio."

## Natural
"By evening, the patio's two or three colors had worn themselves out."

## Grammar Skeleton
Preterite reflexive verb of state-change (`se cansaron`) with an inanimate subject. The colors did not become tired *by* the evening — they tired *themselves* in its company. This is the Spanish habit of pressing a reflexive on what English would write as a passive or a participle. Note also the casual `dos o tres` standing in for "a small countable few," which sounds wrong in English ("two or three colors") but is idiomatic in Spanish.

## Practice Move
Take the structure `Con la tarde se cansaron X de Y` and substitute. *Con la noche se cansaron los pájaros del balcón.* *Con la mañana se despertaron los olores de la cocina.* Keep the reflexive verb and the inanimate subject. Five attempts, no editing.
