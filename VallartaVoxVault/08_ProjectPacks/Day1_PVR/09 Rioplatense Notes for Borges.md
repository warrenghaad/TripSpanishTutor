# 09 Rioplatense Notes for Borges

> Rioplatense Spanish belongs to Buenos Aires, the river, and Borges. **Do not bring it to the PVR airport.**

This note exists so you recognize Rioplatense when you read Borges or hear an Argentine voice — not so you speak it in Mexico.

## What Rioplatense changes

| Feature | Rioplatense (Buenos Aires) | Mexican / neutral (PVR) |
|---|---|---|
| You (singular, informal) | **vos** (*vos tenés*) | tú (*tú tienes*) |
| *ll* and *y* sound | **sh / zh** (*calle* → "ká-sheh") | *y* (*calle* → "ká-yeh") |
| Imperative (tú/vos) | *tené*, *mirá*, *vení* | *ten*, *mira*, *ven* |
| Intonation | sing-song, Italian-influenced | flatter, more clipped |
| Slang | lunfardo (*che*, *boludo*, *laburo*) | mexicanismos (*chido*, *mande*, *ahorita*) |

## When you read Borges

You'll see *vos* almost never in Borges himself (he wrote in a high register), but you'll hear it everywhere in Buenos Aires recordings. Treat *vos* as the spoken Argentine *tú*.

## When *not* to use Rioplatense

- At the PVR airport.
- Ordering tacos.
- Asking the hotel clerk anything.
- Speaking to anyone in Mexico, ever, for Day 1 purposes.

## Voice note

Loving Rioplatense doesn't mean speaking Rioplatense. The workbook keeps the two labs separate.
