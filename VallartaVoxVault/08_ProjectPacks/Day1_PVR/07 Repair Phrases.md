# 07 Repair Phrases

When the conversation breaks, these put it back together without losing your voice.

### I have the idea, but I'm missing the word

Spanish: Tengo la idea, pero me falta la palabra.

English: I have the idea, but I'm missing the word.

Literal: I-have the idea, but to-me lacks the word.

Grammar skeleton: *me falta + [singular noun].* Plural: *me faltan + [plural noun].*

When to use: Any time you stall mid-thought. Buys you patience and respect at once.

Practice:
- Tengo la pregunta, pero me falta la palabra.
- Tengo la imagen, pero me falta el verbo.

Voice note: This is the workbook's flagship phrase. Use it more than you think you should.

---

### I can say it simply, but the idea isn't simple

Spanish: Lo puedo decir simple, pero la idea no es simple.

English: I can say it simply, but the idea isn't simple.

Literal: It I-can to-say simple, but the idea not is simple.

Grammar skeleton: *Lo puedo + [infinitive] + simple, pero ...*

When to use: When you simplify on purpose. Lets the other person know you're choosing simple words, not running out of them.

Practice:
- Lo puedo decir simple, pero estoy pensando en algo más grande.

Voice note: This phrase quietly preserves your adult intelligence inside beginner Spanish.

---

### How do you say ...?

Spanish: ¿Cómo se dice ...?

English: How do you say ...?

Literal: How one-says ...?

Grammar skeleton: *¿Cómo se dice + [word in your language or a gesture]?*

When to use: When you need the Spanish word and want them to teach you.

Practice:
- ¿Cómo se dice *backpack*?
- ¿Cómo se dice esto? *[point at something]*

Voice note: Pointing while asking is fine and encouraged.

---

### What does ... mean?

Spanish: ¿Qué significa ...?

English: What does ... mean?

Literal: What it-means ...?

Grammar skeleton: *¿Qué significa + [word]?*

When to use: When you heard a word you didn't know.

Practice:
- ¿Qué significa "ahorita"?
- ¿Qué significa "mande"?

Voice note: *ahorita* in Mexico can mean *right now* or *eventually*. Ask. Save the answer in [[10 Day 1 Question Catcher]].

---

### One moment, please

Spanish: Un momento, por favor.

English: One moment, please.

Literal: One moment, please.

Grammar skeleton: standalone phrase.

When to use: To buy yourself two seconds to think without panicking.

Practice:
- Un momento, estoy pensando.
- Un momentito, por favor. *(softer / friendlier diminutive)*

Voice note: *un momentito* is warmer in Mexico — the *-ito* makes it polite and small.
