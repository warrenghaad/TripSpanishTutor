# 01 Airport Survival

### Where is the gate?

Spanish: ¿Dónde está la puerta?

English: Where is the gate?

Literal: Where is the door/gate?

Grammar skeleton: *¿Dónde está + [la/el + noun]?*

When to use: Finding any gate, doorway, or specific location at the airport.

Practice:
- ¿Dónde está la salida?
- ¿Dónde está el baño?
- ¿Dónde está el taxi?

Voice note: "puerta" works for *gate* in airport context. *La puerta cinco* = gate five.

### Where is the bathroom?

Spanish: ¿Dónde está el baño?

English: Where is the bathroom?

Literal: Where is the bathroom?

Grammar skeleton: *¿Dónde está + el baño?*

When to use: Any time, anywhere.

Practice:
- ¿Hay un baño cerca?
- ¿El baño está por aquí?

Voice note: In Mexico, *el baño* is the everyday word. Don't overthink it.

---

### I need help

Spanish: Necesito ayuda.

English: I need help.

Literal: I-need help.

Grammar skeleton: *Necesito + [noun].*

When to use: When you really need a human to pause and help.

Practice:
- Necesito un taxi.
- Necesito agua.
- Necesito mi maleta.

Voice note: Short and clear beats polite-and-tangled when you're stuck.

---

### I am lost

Spanish: Estoy perdido.

English: I am lost.

Literal: I-am lost.

Grammar skeleton: *Estoy + [adjective of state].* Use **estar**, not **ser**, for temporary states.

When to use: When you've physically lost the gate, the exit, the friend, the bag.

Practice:
- Estoy cansado.
- Estoy confundido.
- Estoy buscando la salida.

Voice note: Saying *estoy perdido* out loud often makes someone walk you to where you need to go.

---

### I have my passport

Spanish: Tengo mi pasaporte.

English: I have my passport.

Literal: I-have my passport.

Grammar skeleton: *Tengo + mi + [noun].*

When to use: Immigration, check-in, or any official asking for documents.

Practice:
- Tengo mi boleto.
- Tengo mi reservación.
- Tengo mi tarjeta.

Voice note: Hand them the thing while you say the phrase. The phrase doesn't have to carry the whole interaction.

---

### I have a backpack / I'm carrying a backpack

Spanish: Llevo una mochila.

English: I'm carrying a backpack.

Literal: I-carry a backpack.

Grammar skeleton: *Llevo + [item].* **llevar** = to carry, to wear, to take along.

When to use: Describing what you have with you at customs or a checkpoint.

Practice:
- Llevo una maleta y una mochila.
- Llevo solo equipaje de mano.

Voice note: *llevar* is one of those quiet workhorse verbs. It covers *carry*, *wear*, and *take along*.

---

### I'm looking for my luggage

Spanish: Busco mi equipaje.

English: I'm looking for my luggage.

Literal: I-look-for my luggage.

Grammar skeleton: *Busco + [noun].* No need for *por*.

When to use: Baggage claim, lost luggage counter.

Practice:
- Busco la puerta cinco.
- Busco un taxi.
- Busco a mi amigo.

Voice note: *Buscar* takes no preposition in Spanish — don't add *por*. *Busco mi maleta*, not *busco por mi maleta*.
