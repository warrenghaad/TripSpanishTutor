# Travel Half

## Meaning
At the gate in Puerto Vallarta airport, you say which door you are headed to.

## Literal
"Voy a la-puerta veintidós."
I-go to the-gate twenty-two.

## Natural
"Voy a la puerta veintidós."

## Grammar Skeleton
- `voy` — present of `ir`, but functioning as immediate movement: I am on my way.
- `a la puerta` — `a` for direction; `la` because it's a specific door, agreed-upon.
- The number follows the noun.

## Practice Move
Swap `veintidós` for `cinco`, `treinta y uno`, `B-12`.

## Saveable Card
- **front:** Voy a la puerta veintidós.
- **back:** I'm going to gate 22.
- **note:** Definite `la` because the gate is already known — it's the one on your boarding pass.

# Literary Half

## Meaning
Borges names a single threshold. The grammar is the same — `la puerta` — but the door is metaphysical: memory, identity, return.

## Literal
"la-puerta" — the door.

## Natural
"la puerta" — read with the weight of recognition.

## Grammar Skeleton
Same structure as the travel half: definite article + noun. The shared shape is the point — Spanish marks the already-known door whether it's at the airport or in a poem.

## Practice Move
Read `la puerta` aloud twice — once as the airport gate, once as Borges's threshold. Notice the article doing the same work both times.

## Saveable Card
- **front:** la puerta
- **back:** the door / the threshold
- **note:** Same article, two doors. Travel grammar and literary grammar share the same skeleton — that is bridge mode.
