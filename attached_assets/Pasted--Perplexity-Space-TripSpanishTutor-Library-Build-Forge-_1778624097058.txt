# Perplexity Space: TripSpanishTutor Library + Build Forge

This space supports the TripSpanishTutor / Vallarta Voz app.

The app is a literary travel-Spanish companion. It teaches beginner Spanish through two braided paths:

1. Practical travel Spanish, especially airport, taxi, hotel, restaurant, and direction scenarios.
2. Borges poetry as a literary grammar field, beginning with Fervor de Buenos Aires and expanding across Borges's life.

The core user is a beginner who learns quickly through pattern, metaphor, and lived context. The app should not feel like drills first. It should feel like walking through a place where grammar is discovered because the learner needs it.

Current build priorities:

## Priority 1: Offline Airport Mode
Create a pre-cached content pack for airport use:
- directions to go somewhere
- listening to directions
- questions staff may ask
- nouns the traveler carries
- verbs the traveler does
- adjectives describing state/location/objects
- polite repair phrases
- emergency/simple help phrases
- conjugation cards for ir, estar, tener, necesitar, querer, poder, llevar, buscar, esperar, tomar, salir, llegar

The content should be structured as reusable scenelets and phrase cards.

## Priority 2: Borges Literary Mode
Create Borges poem learning scaffolds:
- poem metadata
- historical/literary context
- target lemmas
- grammar patterns
- etymology notes
- cultural notes
- personal echo prompts
- short excerpt-based grammar notes only where appropriate

Do not rely on reproducing full copyrighted poems. The learner has the physical book. The app should scaffold around the text.

## Priority 3: Bridge Mode
Connect practical Spanish to literary Spanish.

Example:
Airport phrase: "¿Dónde está la puerta?"
Borges bridge: puerta as threshold, passage, boundary, fate, entrance into another order.
Grammar bridge: estar for location, noun gender, definite article, question word dónde.

Output requested from this space should be structured in JSON where possible, matching the app's content pack model.
