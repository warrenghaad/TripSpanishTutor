That sounds like a clever twist: a “magnetic poetry” interface where you physically arrange words to feel verb tenses in your hands. Here’s a structured way to turn that idea into a usable feature, plus variations for regular vs. irregular verbs and Spanish-specific practice.

## 🧭 Concept & Overview
Imagine draggable tiles for subjects, verbs, tense markers, and complements. Learners snap tiles into a sentence rail and the app instantly validates the conjugation, hints irregular patterns, and plays audio. It’s tactile, visual, and forgiving — perfect for building muscle memory for tenses.

Here’s a quick breakdown of how it could work.

---

## 🔍 Core Mechanics
- Tiles set
  - Subjects: yo, tú, él/ella/usted, nosotros, vosotros, ellos/ustedes
  - Verb stems: hablar, comer, vivir, ir, ser, tener, hacer, poder, querer…
  - Tense chips: Presente, Pretérito, Imperfecto, Futuro, Condicional, Subjuntivo
  - Irregular hints: small badges like “yo-go”, “stem-change (e→ie)”, “irregular preterite”
  - Complement tiles: en la playa, con mis amigos, hoy, ayer, mañana…

- Snap rail + validation
  - Drop order: Subject → Verb → Tense → Complement.
  - On drop, app autoconjugates and shows the full rendered sentence.
  - If the verb is irregular in that tense, show a subtle tooltip: “hacer → hice (pretérito)”.

- Immediate feedback
  - Color signal: green for correct form; amber for close-but-off (e.g., regular ending used for an irregular).
  - Micro-hints: “Preterite of tener is tuve, not tene…”
  - Audio button for native pronunciation of the full sentence.

- Practice modes
  1. Free Play: build any sentence; validation is passive.
  2. Challenge: you’re given a prompt (e.g., “Pretérito, yo, hacer”), and your tiles must produce “yo hice…”.
  3. Timed Drill: produce 5 forms in 60 seconds.
  4. Minimal Pairs: contrast near-confusions (“yo fui” vs “yo era”; “pude” vs “podía”).

---

## 🧠 Regular vs. Irregular Strategy
- Regular verbs
  - Endings cheat strips appear when hovering over tense chips:
    - -ar (presente): o, as, a, amos, áis, an
    - -er (presente): o, es, e, emos, éis, en
    - -ir (presente): o, es, e, imos, ís, en
  - For preterite, imperfect, etc., show their respective endings on demand.

- Irregular verbs
  - Tag tiles with pattern badges:
    - “yo-go” (tengo, hago, pongo…)
    - stem-change (e→ie, o→ue, e→i) with a mini heatmap highlighting the forms affected.
    - suppletives (ser/ir preterite: fui, fuiste, fue…; tener preterite: tuve, tuviste…)
  - When an irregular tense is selected, the app auto-fills the correct form and explains the rule in one line.

- Contrast coach
  - If a user uses a regular ending on an irregular, show: “Close! Preterite of poder uses ‘pud-’: pude, pudiste, pudo…”

## 🎯 Spanish-in-Travel Context (Vallarta flair)
- Context packs: “Ordering food”, “Directions”, “Small talk”, “Beach day”.
- Complement tiles tuned to travel situations: “una mesa para dos”, “al malecón”, “en efectivo”, “a las siete”.
- Ready-made prompts:
  - “Tell the waiter what you ordered yesterday (pretérito).”
  - “Describe ongoing beach activities (imperfecto).”
  - “Ask politely using present subjunctive: ‘Quiero que…’ + verb.”

## 🧩 UI Implementation Outline (React)
- Data model
  - verbs.json with metadata: infinitive, type (-ar/-er/-ir), stem changes by tense, fully irregular maps.
  - tenses.json: name, endings for regular patterns, irregular note flags.
- Components
  - Tile: draggable; shows label + badges.
  - Rail: droppable slots; renders the final sentence.
  - Conjugator service: given subject, verb, tense → returns form, flags irregularity, provides hint.
  - Feedback strip: color + one-line explanation + audio.
- Interaction niceties
  - Ghost preview: hover over tense to preview conjugations per subject.
  - Double-click verb tile to cycle through tenses quickly.
  - Keyboard fallback: numbers select slots; arrows rotate through verbs.

## 🔬 Example Learning Flows
- Flow A: Preterite sprint
  1. Prompt: “yo + hacer + Pretérito”
  2. Learner builds tiles → app renders “yo hice…” with green feedback.
  3. Next prompt: “nosotros + poder + Pretérito” → “pudimos…”

- Flow B: Imperfect storytelling
  1. Prompt: “ellos + ir + Imperfecto + al malecón”
  2. Tiles → “ellos iban al malecón” with a tooltip: “imperfect: ongoing/used to.”

- Flow C: Subjunctive request
  1. Prompt: “Quiero que + tú + llegar + Presente Subjuntivo”
  2. Tiles → “Quiero que tú llegues” with note: “-ar subjunctive: e, es, e, emos, éis, en.”

## 📈 Progress & Mastery
- Track accuracy by tense and verb class (regular vs. irregular).
- Heatmap of trouble verbs (ser/ir preterite, tener preterite, stem-changes).
- “Next best drill” suggests 3 verbs and 1 tense you miss often.
